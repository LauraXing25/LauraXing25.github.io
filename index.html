Hello, I'm Laura ✳

I am an M.S. student in Mechanical Engineering at Columbia University, where I work with Prof. Matei Ciocarlie in the Robotic Manipulation and Mobility (ROAM) Lab.

My research focuses on large-area robotic skin, tactile sensing, embedded electronics, and robotic perception. I'm particularly interested in how robots perceive and interact with the physical world through touch. I enjoy working across different layers of robotic sensing, from sensor design and electronics to how robots interpret and respond to physical contact.

Previously, I earned my B.S. in Biomedical Engineering at Johns Hopkins University, where I worked with Prof. Nitish Thakor on scalable tactile sensing and compressed sampling, and with Prof. Andrea Cox on the role of LRRK2 in inflammasome activation. These experiences shaped how I approach experimental research across engineering and biomedical science.

JOHNS HOPKINS UNIVERSITY · OCT 2023–AUG 2025

Scalable Tactile Sensing via Compressed Sensing

Neuroengineering & Biomedical Instrumentation Lab · Advisor: Prof. Nitish Thakor

As tactile sensors cover larger surfaces, wiring, communication, and data acquisition become increasingly complicated. With SPTS, we explored how to make large-scale tactile sensing more efficient without adding unnecessary hardware complexity. I designed and investigated a distributed flexible tactile array and compressed sensing framework to reduce sampling demands while preserving contact localization. The project combines ATtiny412-based sensor hardware, embedded communication, and sparse reconstruction, with acquisition at a 60 kHz sampling rate.

COLUMBIA UNIVERSITY · AUG 2025–PRESENT

LEAPS: Large-area Environment-aware PVDF Skin for Rapid Dynamic Sensing

ROAM Lab · Advisor: Prof. Matei Ciocarlie

Robots can precisely track their own movements, but detecting unexpected physical contact across their surfaces remains a challenge. I'm interested in making whole-body tactile sensing practical for safer, more responsive robotic interaction. I'm developing flexible PVDF-based tactile skin with low-noise readout electronics and high-rate sensing to detect transient contact across robot surfaces. My work explores sensitivity tradeoffs, robust contact detection, and responsive robot safety behaviors, alongside soft-robot fabrication and wireless integration.

JOHNS HOPKINS SCHOOL OF MEDICINE · AUG 2022–MAY 2023

LRRK2 and Inflammasome Activation

Division of Infectious Diseases · Advisor: Prof. Andrea Cox

Before working in robotics, I studied the role of LRRK2 in inflammasome activation at Johns Hopkins School of Medicine. Although the research questions were quite different from those I explore today, this experience introduced me to experimental research and the importance of carefully interpreting measurements. I investigated LRRK2's involvement in NLRP3 inflammasome assembly and caspase-1 activation, using cell culture, sample preparation, and western blotting.
